The checklist

Everything the city expects from your building's bins, line by line

The rules live across half a dozen city pages. This is the same list in plain words. Each line links the official page it comes from.

Checked against NYC & DSNY sources · Last checked November 5, 2026

The rule

Containers: Residential buildings with 1 to 9 units must set out trash in official NYC Bins. This took effect June 1, 2026. Recycling and compost go out in sturdy bins with secure lids, 55 gallons or less.

The deadline: The official-bin requirement began June 1, 2026. DSNY issued warnings first; full enforcement began September 8, 2026.

Enforcement: Summonses start at $50 for a first violation, $100 for a second, and $200 for each one after. They are written against the property.

What you actually need to do

  1. Confirm every bin is the right bin. Trash at a 1-9 unit building goes in the official NYC Bin. Recycling and compost each need their own lidded bin, 55 gallons or less. Walk the property once and count.
  2. Write the address on every official bin. Each NYC Bin has a space for the address - the city includes it, in its words, "for easy identification." A printed tag holds up where marker fades.
  3. Label the recycling and compost bins. DSNY requires recycling bins to be clearly labeled. Same standard for compost: a lidded bin, plainly marked.
  4. Mark the storage area (4+ units). In buildings with four or more units, waste storage areas and the bins in them must be clearly identified and labeled. A sign on the bin room door and a tag on each bin covers it.
  5. Have a plan for missing bins. If an official bin is stolen, file a police report and replace it - DSNY does not replace stolen bins. A tagged bin with your address is far more likely to wander back.

Having trouble?

Tenants mix the streams? Labels that say what goes in each bin, not just the address, cut the sorting errors that turn into contamination tickets.

Bins keep disappearing? An address tag won't stop a determined thief, but it ends accidental pickup by the neighbor's super, which is most of the problem.

Not sure how many bins you need? Count one trash bin per pickup's worth of bags, plus one each for recycling and compost, then tag the set as a group ("Bin 1 of 3") so a stray is easy to spot.
